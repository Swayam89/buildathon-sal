An AI company where no agent can make up an email or spend a rupee.

Corporate AI gives AI agents an org chart, a shared task board and rules written in code. JARVIS, a Claude agent, runs it as CEO through MCP. Twenty-four staff agents research, price and write to real suppliers. Money waits for you.

We ran 25 agents as a company. This is where it broke.

  1. Contacts nobody could check. An agent hands you twenty supplier emails. Checking each one by hand takes longer than the research did, so you either trust all of them or none.
  2. The same task, three times. Agents re-assign each other work that already exists, then split it into sub-tasks of sub-tasks.
  3. Promises made with your money. A friendly email to a supplier can confirm an order or offer an advance.
  4. Orders hidden in web pages. A supplier's page can carry invisible text telling agents to ignore their rules.

A better prompt fixes none of these. A prompt is a suggestion.

JARVIS plans. Naina routes. Twenty-four staff do the work.

  1. You give JARVIS an idea. It starts a venture and plans it, or asks Naina, the chief of staff, to turn it into a task graph with dependencies.
  2. One step at a time, the right person works. The most urgent ready task goes to the agent whose job fits: Rehan finds suppliers, Rhea prices the box, Kiara writes the pitch, Navya checks FSSAI.
  3. Everything they propose goes through the rails. New tasks, spending and outreach are checked by code before anything is saved.
  4. Every night, a board report. What's done, what's stuck, what needs your decision, and what the rails stopped.

Prompts are suggestions. These are rules.

Every contact is checked on the page it came from

An email or phone number is used only if it's printed on the page the agent cites. The proof is saved with the draft. A page that won't load can't verify anything, so it's blocked.

Verified manager@assamteasellers.in
on assamteasellers.in: “You may mail us at manager@assamteasellers.in any time”

The company never pays

Costs become requests. Only you can approve them. JARVIS has no tool for it, and messages that promise an order or an advance are blocked.

Every message says it's from an AI

Disclosure is added in code, and cold emails carry an unsubscribe line.

Web pages are data, never orders

Instructions hidden in a page are flagged and ignored. Hidden text and comments never count as contact details.

No duplicate or endless work

Unknown staff, near-duplicate tasks, missing dependencies and sub-tasks more than three levels deep are refused, with a reason the agent can read.

Checking that an email is on a page sounds like one line of code.

The real web disagreed. These came up today, on real supplier sites:

  • A broken tag ate the page. A tea estate's site has a </script tag with no closing bracket. A naive HTML strip swallowed 44 KB of the page, email included. We now end tags the way browsers do.
  • Sites stall bots. 3 of 5 supplier pages timed out on our first try. Fetching them the way a browser does fixed two.
  • Emails hidden as hex. Many business sites hide addresses behind Cloudflare's email protection. We decode them.
  • Unreadable isn't fake. A page that won't load is reported separately from a made-up contact, and both are blocked.
